# Concept Art Catalog — Visual Analysis

This document catalogs the visual assets in `art/concept-art/curated/ig/` and `art/cover/ig/`, providing thematic analysis, palette notes, mood descriptors, and recommended use cases for each image.

## Collection structure

The collection contains **26 images** across two folders:

- `art/concept-art/curated/ig/` — 13 images, odd-numbered sequence (`1.png`–`25.png`)
- `art/cover/ig/` — 13 images, even-numbered sequence (`2.png`–`26.png`)

All images are **1080 × 1350 px** (4:5 portrait, Instagram ratio).

### Pairing convention

Each consecutive odd/even pair (`1/2`, `3/4`, `5/6`, … `25/26`) shares the **same composition** with subtle color-grading differences (mean absolute pixel difference of 1–4 values per channel). The collection therefore represents **13 unique scenes**, each presented in two treatment variants.

Treatment differences between variants are minimal — typically a slight shift in color temperature or saturation (≤ 2 units per channel average). Both variants of each pair are suitable for the same use cases.

### Scene 4 — High Camp at Sunset

**Files:** `concept-art/ig/7.png` · `cover/ig/8.png`

**Style:** Photorealistic with painterly grain/texture overlay  
**Subject:** High-altitude camp — three to four yellow dome tents on a snowy/rocky platform. Lone climber in red/orange jacket and dark pants, viewed from behind, standing among the tents. A dark rocky outcrop frames the left edge of the composition.  
**Mountain:** Snow-covered Andean peaks stretch to the horizon below the camp, confirming very high altitude (likely Cólera or Nido de Cóndores level). Multiple ridgelines visible in atmospheric recession.  
**Light:** Sunset or sunrise. Cool blue-lavender sky (warmth −76 in upper zone) transitioning to warm amber ground (warmth +81 in lower zone). The camp and figure sit at the thermal boundary.  
**Mood:** Rest and preparation. The climber surveys the terrain below. The tents anchor human presence against the vast alpine environment. There is stillness — the decision to advance hasn't been made yet.  
**Palette:** Cool blue-lavender sky · coral-pink horizon · warm amber rock · yellow tent accents · orange jacket · dark rock silhouette  
**Design alignment:** "Climate as a persistent condition." The camp shows human adaptation to altitude — temporary, deliberate, small against the landscape. The cool-warm transition maps directly to the project's environmental pressure gradient.  
**Recommended use:** Camp/rest decision screens, sleep/acclimatize moments, resource management context. Strong for mid-expedition state display. The yellow tents provide a natural accent point against the cold palette.

### Scene 5 — Whiteout (Snow Immersion)

**Files:** `concept-art/ig/9.png` · `cover/ig/10.png`

**Style:** Digital illustration, near-monochrome painterly treatment with fine grain  
**Subject:** High-altitude snowfield in near-whiteout conditions. The entire frame is dominated by snow and diffuse light. A barely discernible figure or accent (0.6% warm spot) may be present but is absorbed into the environment. The foreground, middle ground, and sky are nearly indistinguishable.  
**Mountain:** Invisible or absorbed into the snow/cloud mass. No distinct summit or ridgeline. The terrain reads as pure altitude — undifferentiated, immersive.  
**Light:** Extremely bright, diffuse, non-directional. Uniform brightness across all zones (211–218). No shadows. Slight rose-pink tint throughout gives the whiteness a living warmth rather than clinical blankness.  
**Mood:** Disorientation. Solitude at maximum. The mountain has become the atmosphere itself. There is no visible route, no visible destination — only the enveloping condition.  
**Palette:** Near-white · rose-pink tint · pale lavender · faint warm undertone  
**Design alignment:** The most extreme expression of "climate as a persistent condition." Visibility is the constraint, not terrain. This is environmental pressure made visible — or rather, made invisible.  
**Recommended use:** Extreme weather events, whiteout conditions, high environmental pressure states. Effective as a full-screen atmospheric overlay or blurred background during critical weather decisions. The very low contrast (std 29) makes it ideal for text overlay without competing with content.

### Scene 13 — Blue Hour (Twilight Snowscape)

**Files:** `concept-art/ig/25.png` · `cover/ig/26.png`

**Style:** Digital illustration, high-contrast cool-dominant treatment  
**Subject:** The most dramatically different scene in the collection. The brightness gradient is **inverted** — dark sky above (brightness 113), bright snow below (brightness 197). The foreground is 78% snow, 10% rock. Small dark mass elements (1.9%) and faint blue/teal accents (0.8%) are scattered across the snow. No clear human figure visible; the landscape is unpopulated.  
**Mountain:** The sky is intensely blue (warmth −96, the coldest value in the collection). This is deep twilight — "blue hour" — when the sky's color temperature plunges below the snow's reflected light. The inverted brightness makes the snow glow brighter than the sky, reversing the usual scale grammar.  
**Light:** Blue hour / deep twilight. The sky radiates cold blue while the snowfield reflects residual light, creating an otherworldly luminosity on the ground. High overall contrast (std 60) from the extreme brightness inversion.  
**Mood:** Alien. The inversion of expected brightness relationships creates an uncanny quality. The mountain at this hour becomes a different place — recognizable but transformed. This is the moment when climbers should be off the mountain.  
**Palette:** Deep cobalt-blue sky · indigo · bright snow-lavender · cold grey rock · pale violet foreground  
**Design alignment:** The inverse of every other scene's hierarchy. Where all other images place warmth below and cold above, this scene reverses the relationship. It communicates temporal pressure — the day is gone, the mountain has changed. Appropriate for late-game and critical-state moments.  
**Recommended use:** Late-hour decision screens, permit window expiry, night/bivouac scenarios, critical environmental pressure states. The inverted palette creates immediate visual distinction from all other scenes, making it effective as a signal that conditions have fundamentally changed. Strong for Part 2 narrative transitions.

## Visual language observations

### Palette consistency
The collection shares a warm-cool tension: amber/coral/orange climber accents against lavender/blue-grey/cool mountain environments. This mirrors the project's design-system accent contrast: `--accent-amber #d9b996` against cold `--bg-*` surfaces. The tension is most extreme in Scene 13 (blue hour, warmth −96) and most resolved in Scene 7 (golden hour, all-warm +45).

### Figure treatment
When human figures appear, they are always viewed **from behind** (Scenes 1, 2, 4) or at a distance that anonymizes them (Scenes 3, 9, 10). This is intentional: the player projects onto the climber. The figure is a vector, not a character portrait. In several scenes (5, 11, 13), figures are absent or nearly invisible — the mountain speaks alone.

### Scale grammar
In every figure-present image, the human occupies the **bottom quarter** of the frame. The mountain and sky occupy the top half or more. This proportion is consistent across all 13 scenes and functions as non-negotiable visual grammar for the project.

### Brightness hierarchy
Twelve of thirteen scenes follow a conventional **bright sky / darker ground** hierarchy. Scene 13 (blue hour) inverts this, with brighter snow below and dark sky above. This inversion makes Scene 13 immediately distinctive and appropriate for critical state changes.

### Temperature gradient
Most scenes follow a **cool-top / warm-bottom** gradient, with the sky registering cooler and the terrain warmer. The gradient range varies:
- Sharp split (Scenes 4, 8, 12): coolest skies (−35 to −76) with warmest ground (+67 to +81)
- All-warm (Scenes 2, 7): the entire frame bathes in warm light
- All-cool (Scene 13): inverted, all-cold palette
- Neutral/diffuse (Scenes 5, 10, 11): minimal gradient, soft atmospheric quality

### Pairing treatment
The concept-art (odd) and cover (even) variants of each scene differ by approximately 1–4 pixel values per channel on average. Differences are most visible in:
- Slight saturation shifts in the sky zone
- Minor warmth adjustments in the ground register
- Occasional micro-contrast differences at highlight edges

Both variants are interchangeable for production use. The pairing allows A/B selection based on subtle tonal preference without compositional change.
