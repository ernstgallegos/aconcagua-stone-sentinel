## One-sentence pitch

*Aconcagua: Stone Sentinel* is a contemplative indie game about reading signals, respecting limits, and making decisions that do not rewind — set on the highest peak outside Asia.

---

## Short description (1 paragraph)

*Aconcagua: Stone Sentinel* is a mountain-first narrative systems game developed as an independent project in Argentina. Players take on one of six expedition roles and attempt to navigate Aconcagua's environmental pressures, route constraints, and physiological realities in a turn-based structure built on a real Environmental Pressure / Body Tolerance model. The summit is one valid outcome among many — safe return, strategic retreat, and permit management carry equal narrative weight. The public web prototype (Part 1) is currently playable at [aconcaguastonesentinel.com](https://aconcaguastonesentinel.com).

## Design pillars

**1. Environmental authority**  
Altitude, weather, route timing, and fatigue load shape every run. The mountain writes the rules; the player responds.

**2. Partial information**  
Signals are interpreted through confidence ranges, not omniscient dashboards. Perception degrades before collapse is visible.

**3. Plural outcomes**  
Summit-only success is an anti-design choice. Strategic retreat and safe return are valid endings with real mechanical and narrative weight.

**4. Contemplation as gameplay**  
Waiting, resting, observing, and choosing not to act are real actions with real consequences.

---

## What the game is not

- Not an action game set in the mountains.
- Not a survival game built around constant failure and punishment.
- Not a "soulslike" or "boss-fight" interpretation of mountaineering.
- Not empty-postcard tourism: the mountain is a system, not a backdrop.

## Interpersonal dynamics

The six characters are designed as an ensemble, not just six solo paths. These tensions are canonical for story framing and expedition dynamics:

- **Francisco ↔ Laura:** shared guilt-adjacent burden; the expedition's most functional trust pair when both are reading clearly.
- **Erik ↔ Blake:** validation tension — technical competence filtering versus status-based overcompensation.
- **Irina ↔ Daniela:** expertise distrust versus observational insight; their dynamic resolves when observation proves mountain-first, not aesthetic.

## The system (EP/BT model)

The game runs on a turn-resolver where:

1. The player chooses an action: **advance**, **advance slowly**, **wait**, **descend**, **sleep**, or (for Daniela) **shoot photo**.
2. **Environmental Pressure (EP)** is calculated from altitude band, weather severity, terrain load, time of day, and cumulative exposure.
3. **Body Tolerance (BT)** integrates fatigue, exposure accumulation, and functional capacity.
4. A **perceived signal** is generated from EP and BT through a character-specific confidence and trend layer — players never see raw values, only interpreted readings with varying reliability.
5. The outcome is classified: progress, hold, retreat, or terminal event.

All parameters are data-driven (JSON files) and all claims are testable. The engine is fully decoupled from the UI.

## The scenarios (v1.5.1)

Five distinct scenarios shape starting conditions, pressure bias, and which danger axis dominates each run:

| # | Name | Difficulty | Core tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Assisted Route** | Low | Learning to read signals before conditions stop being forgiving |
| 02 | **Narrow Weather Window** | Medium | Racing a deteriorating atmospheric trend |
| 03 | **False Stability Terrain** | Medium | Clear skies hiding progressive terrain load buildup |
| 04 | **Accumulated Fatigue Trap** | Medium | Benign conditions masking compounding physical debt |
| 05 | **Weather Window** | Hard | A brief clearing in severe weather — recognize when it opens and closes |

Scenarios share the same route nodes and outcome system. They differ in initial state (fatigue, resources, weather severity) and environmental bias (whether conditions tend to deteriorate, stabilize, or briefly improve).

## The outcomes (canonical)

The game has ten terminal outcomes. All are valid; none are exclusively "failure."

| Outcome | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Summit and Safe Return** | Reached the summit and descended to park exit |
| **High Point Return** | Turned back from altitude without incident; the mountain sets the limit |
| **Strategic Retreat** | Deliberate retreat before conditions became critical — a correct read |
| **Rescue** | Deterioration required external intervention |
| **Collapse (Fatigue)** | Physical fatigue exceeded body tolerance threshold |
| **Collapse (Exposure)** | Cumulative environmental exposure exceeded body tolerance |
| **Resource Exhaustion** | Water or food depleted; descent could not be sustained |
| **Expedition Window Closed** | Permit days expired before exit |
| **Permit Expired** | Calendar deadline reached before park exit |
| **Fatality** | Maximum pressure threshold crossed with no recovery margin |

Summit and Safe Return is the rarest outcome — typically 10–30% for human players who internalize the EP/BT system. Strategic Retreat is the most common non-collapse outcome and is explicitly designed to feel like a win.

## Technical transparency

- **381 automated tests** covering engine, UI helpers, API, and contract parity.
- **Monte Carlo simulation**: 1,500 headless runs validate structural integrity. No 0% summit rates across any character.
- **Open source**: full repository is public and auditable.
- **5 scenarios**: Assisted Route, Narrow Weather Window, False Stability Terrain, Accumulated Fatigue Trap, Weather Window.
- **10 route nodes** across 3 stages: Approach, High Camp, Summit Day.

## Cultural commitment

Aconcagua is not only a record to break. It is a territory layered with Andean cultural presence, ancestral routes, and Indigenous cosmological significance. The project takes a clear stance: no empty folklore, no ornamental local color. Human presence appears through traces, context, and carefully researched references — or not at all.
